Starting an LLC in Georgia

Step-by-step plan, official filings, costs, and timeline

Why LLC: Separates your personal assets from business debts/lawsuits, simple pass-through taxation (profits taxed on your personal return, no separate corporate tax), and lighter paperwork than a corporation. Good default for most small businesses.

1Choose & Check Your Business Name

Cost: Free (optional reservation $25)Time: Same day
  • Search name availability at Georgia Corporations Division business search (sos.ga.gov).
  • Name must include "LLC," "L.L.C.," or "Limited Liability Company" and be distinguishable from existing GA entities.
  • Optional: reserve the name for 30 days ($25) if you're not ready to file yet.
  • Check the name isn't trademarked (USPTO.gov) and the matching domain/social handles are available.

2Appoint a Registered Agent

Cost: $0 (self) or $50–150/yr (service)
  • Required by law — a person or company with a physical Georgia street address (no P.O. boxes) who accepts legal/tax mail on your behalf during business hours.
  • You can be your own registered agent if you have a GA address, or hire a commercial registered agent service for privacy.

3File Articles of Organization

Cost: $100 online / $110 by mailTime: ~7 business days online
  • File through the Georgia Corporations Division's eCorp online portal (sos.ga.gov).
  • You'll need: business name, registered agent name/address, principal office address, organizer's name.
  • This filing officially creates your LLC.

4Create an Operating Agreement

Cost: Free (DIY) or $0–150 (template/lawyer)
  • Not filed with the state, but strongly recommended — even for a single-member LLC.
  • Defines ownership %, how profits are split, how decisions are made, and what happens if a member leaves.
  • Banks and courts often ask for it to confirm the LLC is legitimately separate from you personally.

5Get an EIN (Employer ID Number)

Cost: FreeTime: Instant online
  • Apply free directly at irs.gov (never pay a third party for this).
  • Needed to open a business bank account, hire employees, and file federal taxes.

6Open a Business Bank Account

Cost: Varies by bank
  • Bring: Articles of Organization, EIN confirmation letter, Operating Agreement, ID.
  • Keeping business and personal money separate is essential to preserve your liability protection.
  • Consider a business credit card for building business credit history.

7Register for Georgia State Taxes

Cost: Free to register
  • Register on the Georgia Tax Center (GTC) — gtc.dor.ga.gov.
  • If you sell taxable goods/services: register for a Sales & Use Tax number and collect Georgia sales tax (state rate 4%, plus local — combined typically 6–8% depending on county).
  • If hiring employees: register for state withholding tax and with the Georgia Department of Labor for unemployment insurance.

8Get Local Business Licenses & Permits

Cost: ~$50–400 (varies by city/county)
  • Apply for a business/occupation tax certificate ("business license") from the city or county where you operate — this is separate from state LLC formation.
  • Check zoning rules if operating from a physical location or home office.
  • Regulated industries (food service, childcare, construction, cosmetology, etc.) need additional state professional licenses — check the Georgia Secretary of State's Professional Licensing Boards.

9Get Business Insurance

Cost: Varies
  • General liability insurance — protects against customer injury/property damage claims.
  • Workers' compensation — legally required in Georgia once you have 3+ employees (including corporate officers in some cases).
  • Consider commercial property and professional liability coverage depending on your industry.

10Set Up Bookkeeping

  • Use accounting software (QuickBooks, Wave, Xero) from day one.
  • Track income/expenses separately per the business bank account — makes tax filing and the annual registration easier.
  • Consider a CPA or bookkeeper once revenue picks up.

11File Your Annual Registration (Ongoing)

Cost: $60/yearDue: Jan 1 – Apr 1 each year
  • Required every year to keep your LLC in good standing with the Georgia Secretary of State.
  • Not required in your formation year — first one is due Jan 1–Apr 1 of the year after you form.
  • Missing Apr 1 adds a $25 late fee; missing Jul 1 risks administrative dissolution of your LLC.

Quick Checklist

  • Business name checked & available
  • Registered agent identified
  • Articles of Organization filed ($100–110)
  • Operating Agreement written
  • EIN obtained from IRS
  • Business bank account opened
  • Registered on Georgia Tax Center (sales tax if applicable)
  • Local business license / occupation tax certificate obtained
  • Business insurance in place
  • Bookkeeping system set up
  • Calendar reminder set for Annual Registration (Jan 1 – Apr 1 next year, $60)
Fees and requirements reflect Georgia Secretary of State and Georgia Dept. of Revenue guidance as of September 2026 and are subject to change — verify current fees at sos.ga.gov and gtc.dor.ga.gov before filing. This document is general guidance, not legal or tax advice.
